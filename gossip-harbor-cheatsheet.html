Harbor cheat sheet

For players past level 117. You know the basics, so this sheet is about the late game: long item chains, expensive orders, and getting the most out of every point of energy. Fair play only: no hacks, modded apps or "free gems" generators.

Energy caps, timers and prices change between game updates and events. Numbers from player guides are rules of thumb, not guarantees.

What changes after level 117

  • Orders get expensive. They ask for high levels in long chains, so one order can cost far more energy than early in the game.
  • Level-ups come slowly. Don't plan your energy around the next level-up. Plan it around events.
  • Events are the main engine. Late in the game they give far more per energy than the main board.
  • The board is the bottleneck. With many producers and half-built chains, space and storage decide how fast you move.

Rule of thumb: every tap should serve an open order, an event goal or a producer upgrade. Anything else is wasted energy.

Energy

Do

  • Merge energy bottles to the top level before you use them. A top-level bottle gives many times more than a low one.
  • Let the free daily energy and coin packs merge up to their highest level before you open them.
  • Keep a stock of bottles for events and hard orders.
  • Spend regenerating energy before the bar is full, and keep the bottles.

Don't

  • Open small energy bottles as soon as you get them.
  • Use x2/x4 energy boosters on chains where no order needs the higher items.
  • Buy energy with gems outside the last hours of an event.
  • Tap producers only to fill the board.

Producers

  • Keep two of each producer type when you can. When one is recharging, the other keeps working.
  • Upgrade the producers behind today's orders first. Upgrading a chain you rarely need costs space for nothing.
  • Orange trees: player guides suggest merging them to about level 4–6 and no further. At that level they give free oranges for the cooking station, which pays coins, energy and gems.
  • Use producers that run out after a set number of taps only for items an order needs.
  • Don't pay gems to skip a recharge unless an event is about to end.

Orders

  • Plan backwards. For a high-level order, count how many level-1 items it takes: every level doubles the count. Then decide whether it is worth the energy now.
  • Do orders that finish a story task or an event goal first.
  • Look for items that fit two orders at once before you merge.
  • Leave expensive orders from chains you haven't built up yet. They will wait.
  • Never merge past the level an order asks for.

Board and storage

  • Late in the game, storage slots are the best long-term gem buy. Chains are long, and half-finished pieces need somewhere to wait.
  • Keep about a third of the board free, so producers and chests have room to drop items.
  • Sell items from chains no open order or event uses.
  • Keep one or two high-level pieces of each common chain in storage. New orders often ask for them.
  • Keep each chain in its own corner of the board so pairs are easy to spot.

Events

  • Save energy bottles, gems and high-level items for a few days before an event you can see coming.
  • Races: join early and spend your saved energy in one burst.
  • Read each event's rules. Many use their own board or tokens, and the tokens don't count on the main board.
  • Work out whether you can reach the last milestone. If not, stop at the milestone with the best reward and save the rest.
  • Claim milestone rewards before the timer ends. Unclaimed rewards can be lost.

Gems

Worth it

  • Extra storage slots
  • Reaching an event milestone just before the timer ends
  • A bubble with a very high-level item that an order needs

Waste

  • Skipping short producer timers
  • Energy outside events
  • Speeding up anything that finishes while you sleep

Keep a gem reserve for the end of events. That is where gems pay the most.

Bubbles

  • High-level merges sometimes spawn a bubble with a bonus copy. Popping it costs gems; if you wait, it turns into coins.
  • Late in the game a bubble can save a lot of energy. Pop it if the item is at a high level and an open order or event needs it.
  • Let it expire if the item is a low level or from a chain you don't use.
